SCNI combines how widely actors can reach one another with how close they are along cooperative paths.

SCNI = reachability × proximity

Reachability is the share of possible actor pairs connected through cooperative paths. Proximity is the mean inverse path length among those reachable pairs.

Inside the ten-week calculation

Each Monday labels a week of reporting. A snapshot combines that week and the nine before it. Actors from every retained interaction type form the window’s roster; cooperative ties form its paths.

A fresh cooperative edge has length 1. Each week adds 0.2; a new cooperative report refreshes it. Weighted shortest paths connect actors indirectly, and unreachable pairs contribute zero to SCNI.

This series is calculated for the showcase’s four scope bands using the regional research formula and its federal/state government-pair exclusion. The faint backdrop retains the wider two-year network.

About this project

Geelong, connected brings research on social cohesion into an explorable network of reported relationships. Follow selected stories to see how community groups, organisations and institutions connect—and read the news passages behind those connections.

The research is conducted by Noah Star and Professor Simon Angus, SoDa Labs, Department of Economics, Monash University, as part of the wider research activities of the Australian Resilient Democracy Network (ARDN).

The accompanying research paper is How do Climate Disasters Affect Social Cohesion? Evidence from a New(s) High Frequency Approach. For a copy or more information, contact simon.angus@monash.edu.

From news to networks: the research approach

Find the reporting. The study draws on 31,704 articles from regional Victorian newspapers in 2015–2016, retrieved through ProQuest TDM Studio using searches for groups and their interactions.

Recover relationships. AI models extract candidate group-to-group ties, check each article’s time and place, and validate the relationships. Ties are classified as cooperative, antagonistic or neutral.

Build the networks. Duplicate ties are removed and actor names are matched to consistent identifiers. Regional networks bring these relationships together. This showcase explores Geelong’s network through selected stories and their news excerpts.

Measure cohesion. The paper’s weekly Social Cohesion Network Index uses cooperative ties in five- and ten-week rolling windows, weighting recent ties more heavily. It combines reachability—how widely groups connect—with proximity—how short the paths between connected groups are—to study how cohesion changes after bushfires and floods.

Explore the weekly index. Network over time reconstructs SCNI for the showcase’s four scope bands, applying the regional code’s federal/state government-pair exclusion. Weekly snapshots contain the current and nine preceding Monday-based reporting weeks. Cooperative paths have edge lengths of 1 + 0.2 × weeks since the latest cooperative report. The mean inverse weighted shortest-path length across every possible pair gives SCNI; actors from all retained interaction types enter its denominator. The timeline starts with nine build-up weeks and marks the 27 January 2016 flood.
